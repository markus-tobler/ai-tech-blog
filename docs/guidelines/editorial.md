# Editorial guidelines

## Purpose

Help readers make better decisions about AI in their organisation. Every article should leave the reader able to decide or do something they could not before.

## Voice

Business decision maker enabler, with depth.

- **First person, from experience.** "In three rollouts I saw…" beats "Organisations often…". Experience must be real: ask the author, never invent it.
- **Educational and balanced.** Explain the why. Show trade-offs and when the opposite choice is right.
- **Decision-supporting.** Name the decision, the options and the criteria. End with a recommendation or next step.
- **Calm and precise.** No hype, no fear. Concrete nouns and verbs. Short sentences, active voice.
- **Respectful of all three audiences.** Leaders get the "so what", citizen developers the "how", architects the "how it fits". Mark deep technical parts so leaders can skip them.

## Audiences

| Value | Reader | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `leaders` | Business and IT decision makers | Impact, risk, cost, operating model, what to decide |
| `citizen-devs` | Power users building with low-code and AI | Practical patterns, guardrails, what they may build |
| `pro-devs` | Developers and architects | Architecture, integration, governance mechanics, code |

## Article types

- **Insight**: one observation from practice, why it matters, what to do. 800–1200 words.
- **Decision guide**: a decision, options, criteria, recommendation. Often with a comparison table. 1200–1800 words.
- **How-to**: step-by-step guide with prerequisites and result. Length as needed.
- **Field note**: short lesson learned from a project. 400–700 words.

## Structure

1. **Title**: says what the reader gets, in sentence case. No clickbait.
2. **Description**: one or two sentences, the promise of the article.
3. **Key takeaways**: three bullets at the top.
4. **Context**: the situation and why it matters now.
5. **Insight or analysis**: the core, with examples and evidence.
6. **Decision aid**: options, criteria, table or checklist.
7. **What to do next**: concrete next steps, per audience if useful.

Headings are sentence case and describe the content ("Why central governance fails"), not labels ("Analysis").

## Evidence and sources

- Link primary sources: vendor documentation, laws, studies, original announcements.
- Date-sensitive facts (features, prices, licensing, regulation) name the date or version.
- Distinguish facts, experience and opinion. "I think" is fine for opinion.
- Quotes must be exact and attributed.

## Confidentiality

Never name clients, employers or colleagues, or reveal internal details, numbers or screenshots. Anonymise by industry and size ("a mid-sized Swiss bank"). When in doubt, leave it out and ask the author.

## AI assistance

AI may help research, outline, draft, edit and translate. The author owns ideas, experience and final wording, and reviews every sentence. Set `aiAssistance` honestly; the site shows a matching note under each article.

## Images and media

- Prefer diagrams that explain over decorative stock images.
- Store images next to the post (`src/content/blog/<lang>/images/`) and give every image alt text that says what it shows.
- Only use images you own or that allow reuse; credit them in a caption.
